# Executive impersonation asking for an urgent payment or favor

## Technique
Business email compromise. The attacker poses as a senior executive or a
trusted partner and asks an employee to make a payment, buy something or share
data, usually while claiming to be unavailable by phone. The request is plain
text with no link, which helps it slip past filters.

## Warning signs
- An unusual request for money or sensitive data from someone senior.
- Pressure for secrecy or speed, with a reason why the person cannot be called.
- A reply-to address that differs from the apparent sender.
- A change in the usual process for approving payments.

## How to verify
Confirm the request through a different channel, such as a phone call to a
known number or a message in an established chat. Payments outside the normal
approval process should always be double-checked.
